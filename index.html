Étienne Thoret.

How does the world become sound, and sound become perception?

I am a CNRS research scientist at the Institut de Neurosciences de la Timone, Aix-Marseille Université. My work investigates how acoustic structure becomes perceptual and neural structure.

I combine psychophysics, auditory neuroscience, sound synthesis, signal processing and interpretable machine learning. From musical timbre and voices to natural soundscapes, I study what information listeners and computational models use to make sense of sounds—and what hearing can reveal about the environment.

Research

Listening, modelling, understanding.

A common thread across my projects is to connect physically measurable sound to the information used by human and artificial auditory systems.

01 /

Human auditory ecology

How do people hear and interpret natural environments? I combine field recordings, ecological observations and perceptual experiments to study the structure of soundscapes, from the Sahel to tropical forests.

Related paper ↗
02 /

Brain representations of sound

Using fMRI, neural recordings and computational models, I examine how auditory regions represent voices, actions, objects and information carried by complex acoustic signals.

Related paper ↗
03 /

Explainable auditory models

What acoustic cues drive a decision? I use reverse correlation, experimental perturbations, auditory representations and machine learning to compare human and artificial listening strategies.

Related paper ↗
04 /

Sound, timbre and adaptive hearing

Musical timbre, biological motion and auditory signal representations provide ways to probe the computational principles that shape listening, including adaptive cascaded envelope interpolation.

Related paper ↗

Projects

Selected research programmes.

Selected funded and collaborative programmes spanning experimental auditory research, computational modelling and environmental sound.

CNRS Mission for Interdisciplinarity · Principal investigator

ReScape — restoring the soundscapes of the Great Green Wall

Research at the intersection of human auditory perception, ecoacoustics and ecosystem biodiversity, developed with ecological and anthropological partners in Senegal.

CNRS International Emerging Action · Principal investigator

Deciphear — decoding auditory representations

Developing reverse-correlation and perturbation approaches to reveal information encoded in deep auditory models, in collaboration with Frédéric Gosselin (Université de Montréal).

ANR · Collaborating investigator

Punk Sound Is Not Dead

An interdisciplinary investigation of punk sound and perception, combining acoustics, machine learning, musicology and cultural research.

ILCB · Principal investigator · 2019–2023

Perceptual representations and interpretable machine learning

Two successive research programmes on auditory metric learning, sound synthesis, computational audition and explainable models of auditory brain processing.
